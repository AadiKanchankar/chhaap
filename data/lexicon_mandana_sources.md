# Mandana lexicon: sources

TASK2 section 10 describes Mandana as "a ritual folk art practised by the Meena community during festivals". The pages below were opened and read. They support most of that sentence. They add two points the sentence leaves out: the painters are women, and Mandana is also drawn for life events such as births and weddings, for a new house, and after whitewashing.

## Who makes Mandana

Rooftop (https://rooftopapp.com/art-wiki/mandana) says "women of the Meena community have long painted the floors and walls of their homes" and "Mandana is painted only by Meena women". It names the Meena districts as Jaipur, Alwar, Bharatpur, Sawai Madhopur and Tonk, and says that in Madhya Pradesh the practice is limited to floor paintings.

The NIOS Folk Art lesson 8 (https://www.nios.ac.in/media/documents/244_Folk_art/Folk_Art_Practical_L-8.pdf) calls Mandana "a traditional folk art of Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh" and says "Only women make them". It also says women learn the craft from their mothers in childhood. It does not name the Meena or any other community.

Oaklores (https://oaklores.com/2024/12/08/mandana-art-delineated-by-geometry-nature-and-religion/) places the tradition in "the homes of the Meena tribe" and says it is "primarily practiced by women", handed down from mothers to daughters.

Chantal Jumel's field notes from Rajasthan show women and girls drawing together. Her Bundi page names the Meena only in a photo caption, and her Jodhpur pages show families who are not identified as Meena (https://chantal-jumel-kolam-kalam.com/en/rajasthan-mandana-part-1-around-jodhpur/). Her part 5 says Mandana designs "vary according to regions, festivals, and communities in Rajasthan" (https://chantal-jumel-kolam-kalam.com/en/rajasthan-mandana-villages-adorning-the-floors-for-diwalii-part-5/).

Search results quoted Memeraki as saying the Bhil and Shrimali Brahmans also practise Mandana. The Memeraki, INHCRF and Utsavpedia pages failed to open (TLS certificate errors), so that claim is unverified and no lexicon row uses those pages.

## When it is made

NIOS lists "auspicious occasions and festivals such as Dussehra, Diwali, Holi", names Diwali as "the main festival of Mandana", and adds Dev Prabodhini Ekadashi, Makar Sankranti, "new house and after whitewash". Rooftop gives "local festivals, religious ceremonies, births, and marriages". Jumel's part 1 says "Birth, marriage, and other Hindu festivals are all reasons to draw" (https://chantal-jumel-kolam-kalam.com/en/rajasthan-mandana-jodhpur-the-quest-begins-part-1/).

## Suggested wording

"Mandana is a ritual floor and wall art of Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh, painted by women and most closely tied to the Meena community. It is drawn for festivals, Diwali above all, and for births, weddings and a new house."

## Motif sources

The NIOS lesson supports the footprints, both lotus rows, the chariot of Lakshmi and the threshold band. Jumel part 5 supports the six-petalled flower, the swastika, the hand fan, the chaupad square and the stepwell square. Jumel's Bundi page (https://chantal-jumel-kolam-kalam.com/en/rajasthan-mandana-part-4-visiting-bundi/) supports the peacock, Jumel part 2 supports the square with Lakshmi's feet, sun and moon, and Oaklores supports the separate sun and moon rows.

Left out for lack of an opened source: stars, and the Tapki dot-grid Mandana (the page that described it returned 404). Devi Jot is in NIOS as the light of earthen lamps, but no page describes its shape, so it is also left out. Oaklores gives a meaning for the kalash (abundance, drawn for weddings and festivals); it was not on the candidate list and was not added.
