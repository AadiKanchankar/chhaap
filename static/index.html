        Print a design to see what its symbols mean.

        Print files are drawn at 300 DPI from vectors, never upscaled. Every file carries the label AI-generated, Warli-inspired. Not a GI-certified Warli painting. in its metadata, and the mockup shows it.

            Where the training images came from

            The style images are the Kaggle Indian paintings dataset (ajg117): eight classes, of which only the Warli class trains the LoRA. The images were scraped from the web and the dataset gives no clear license. Chhaap treats them as research use only, and a held-out fifth of the Warli images is never trained on.

            Who owns Warli

            Warli painting received a Geographical Indication in 2014. A GI protects the name and the community's claim on the craft: a print from this tool is not a Warli painting and must not be sold as one. Who counts as the community is contested. Gond painting received its GI in April 2023, awarded to two organisations in Dindori district; Bhopal-based Gond artists and the family of Jangarh Singh Shyam objected to being left off the applicants and wrote to the GI registry in January 2024 (Priti David, PARI, April 2024).

            Mandana, and mixing the two

            Mandana is a ritual floor and wall art of Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh. The NIOS folk art lesson says only women make it, for festivals with Diwali first among them, and for a new house; Rooftop ties it to the women of the Meena community. Its motifs here come from those pages and from Chantal Jumel's field notes. A design that puts a Mandana border around a Warli centre is fusion, a contemporary design practice and not a traditional form: the two arts come from different communities and regions. Such files say so in their label. No Mandana style adapter has been trained yet, so Mandana designs are painted without one.

            Why outputs are labelled

            Generated images can pass for hand work. Every export says it is AI-generated and Warli-inspired, so a buyer is not misled about who made it.

            Who decides what goes where

            The layout engine places every symbol and the palette colours every layer. A language model, when one is used, only turns the brief into a plan; it never draws and never writes a meaning. Diffusion adds the hand-painted line, and a survival check confirms each placed symbol is still there.

            Where the meanings come from

            Each symbol in the lexicon links to a published source. No Warli artist has checked them yet. The deployment model proposed for this project is a LoRA licensed from Warli artists, with royalties per use; it has not been tested.
